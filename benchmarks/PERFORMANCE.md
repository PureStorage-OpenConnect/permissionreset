# Performance Results

Last updated: 2026-08-17

## Environment

- **Array**: FlashBlade S500, data VIP 192.0.2.50
- **Mount**: `/mnt/fb-perms` (filesystem `fb-perms`)
- **Mount options**: `vers=4.1,nconnect=16,noatime,nodiratime`
- **Client**: 12-core Linux VM, 8 GB RAM
- **Go**: 1.25.0
- **Workers**: 128 (default)
- **Binary**: statically linked, no cgo

## Pre-pass scaling

The pre-pass fixes directory permissions top-down before enumeration. Its cost is dominated by readdir round-trips over NFS, not CPU.

| Dataset | Directories | Pre-pass time | Dirs/sec |
|---|---|---|---|
| bigdata (1.1M) | 104,409 | 3.1s | 33,680 |
| testdata5 (46K) | 6,409 | 0.2s | 32,045 |
| testdata4 (6K) | 1,256 | 0.1s | 12,560 |

Pre-pass does not improve past 128 workers — bound by NFS metadata round-trips per directory.

## Tree generation — generate-tree (Go)

Go tree generator with named-principal NFSv4 ACLs. Replaces `generate_tree_windows.py` for large-scale test data.
Uses direct setxattr via `internal/nfs4acl`, 64 workers, 15 scenarios (11 dir, 4 file).

| Scale | Files | ACL paths | Fan-out | ACL stamp | Total | ACL rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small | ~500 | 370 | 0.1s | 0.2s | 0.6s | 2,065/s |
| medium | ~5,000 | 2,900 | 0.2s | 1.1s | 1.7s | 2,549/s |
| large | ~40,000 | 20,600 | 1.1s | 6.5s | 8.4s | 3,184/s |
| xlarge | ~1,000,000 | 1,155,000 | 20.1s | 19.3s | 37.9s | 63,875/s |
| huge | ~100,000,000 | 111,069,160 | — | 1735.3s | 1735.3s | 64,024/s |

Every file is stamped explicitly (NFSv4 inheritance does not apply retroactively to existing files).
xlarge/huge rates reflect pipelined walk+stamp with pre-encoded ACL bytes.

### Userspace transport — generate-tree (2026-08-30)

Userspace NFSv4.1 transport with in-memory seed planning (`planSeedEntries`), 8 connections, 128 workers, Purity//FB 4.8.4 on S500. Create + stamp in a single pass — no separate fan-out or seed-build phase.

| Scale | Files | Applied | Seed | Fan-out+stamp | Total | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| huge | ~100,000,000 | 111,019,160 | 0.0s | 1472.7s | 1472.7s | 75,387/s |

**1.18x faster than kernel** (75,387 vs 64,024 paths/sec). Create+stamp combined vs kernel stamp-only. Zero failures, 15/15 scenarios, manifest written locally via `--manifest`.

Connection sweep (500 copies, 2K seed, depth 14 — same per-subtree density as huge):

| Connections | Rate (paths/s) | Elapsed |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 41,836 | 26.2s |
| 4 | 32,562 | 32.1s |
| 8 | 23,238 | 31.9s |
| 16 | 10,798 | 32.4s |
| 32 | 826 | 32.3s |

At small copy counts c=1 wins — more connections add session coordination overhead without
enabling more parallelism (128 workers already saturate, each processes one subtree serially).
At huge scale (50K copies), c=8 edges ahead by ~4.5% because directory creation parallelism
amortizes across the longer run:

| Connections | Rate (paths/s) | Elapsed | vs kernel |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 72,164 | 25.6 min | 1.13x |
| 4 | 74,774 | 24.7 min | 1.17x |
| 8 | 75,387 | 24.5 min | 1.18x |

**c=8 default is correct for generate-tree at scale.** All beat kernel (64K/s, 28.9 min).
Diminishing returns past c=4 — only 0.8% slower than c=8.

Raw data: `generate-tree-huge-userspace-20260830/` (c=8), `generate-tree-huge-userspace-c1-20260831/` (c=1),
`generate-tree-huge-userspace-c4-20260831/` (c=4), `generate-tree-conn-sweep-20260831/` (connection sweep).

Comparison: `generate_tree_windows.py` at large scale (40K files) takes ~28 min over SMB. Go generator: 8.4s.

## Extrapolations

Single client, single NFS mount, no parallelism across clients. Anchored to measured 110M result.

| Scale | Apply (estimated) | Validate (estimated) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 110M | **28 min (measured)** | — | 82K main, 66K overall |
| 1B | ~4.2 hours | ~3.2 hours | Based on 66K overall ops/sec |
| 10B | ~42 hours | ~32 hours | Single client bound |

Pre-pass scaling: 343s for 13.5M dirs at 110M = ~39K dirs/sec. At 1B (~135M dirs): ~58 min pre-pass.
Main pass: 82K ops/sec held steady from 1.1M through 110M — no degradation with scale.
